# Close

Section 8 of [[Talk Outline]], the ending. Keep it short. A close that runs long undoes the energy the demos built. The job here is to land one message, give one action, and open the floor.

## Slide 1. The arc in one line

**Slide:** You came in with a backlog you had no hours for. You leave able to clear it.

Speaker notes:
- Recap the shape of the talk in three beats, fast: what the tool is, what it does in security work, and how to use it without hurting yourself.
- Name the shift again for the room. Large teams got faster. You got work that did not exist before, because the script never got written and now it does.

## Slide 2. The one thing to remember

**Slide:** A fast junior analyst. Capable, tireless, and never trusted blind.

Speaker notes:
- If they forget everything else, this is the frame that keeps them safe and productive. It carries the capability and the discipline in one picture.
- Restate the five guardrails in a breath: read before you run, work in a safe place, protect the data, keep permissions tight, verify the output.

## Slide 3. The one thing to do

**Slide:** Monday, pick one annoying task. Give it one hour.

Speaker notes:
- The whole talk collapses to this. Not a project. One task, one hour, in a scratch folder or on a copy of the data.
- Remind them the first hour is slower than doing it by hand, and the second run wins it back. The point is to start, not to be efficient on day one.
- If they have no paid plan, the free OpenRouter path from the setup section means cost is not an excuse today.

## Slide 4. Take the material with you

**Slide:** The whole talk, the setup steps, and the prompts, in one page.

Speaker notes:
- Point them at the handout. The [[Cite Talk Guide]] is a single page that collects the setup, the example prompts, the guardrails, and the resource list. The [[Setup Guide]] is the step-by-step install, and [[Resource Index]] is the longer list of tools.
- Tell them where to get it: the repository link or the shared location, whichever you are using on the day. Fill in the actual link before the talk.

## Slide 5. Thanks and questions

**Slide:** Thank you. What would you want it to do first?

Speaker notes:
- Thank the room and the organizers.
- Open with a question that gets hands up, not silence: ask what task in their own week they would point it at first. It surfaces real use cases and warms up the Q&A.
- Have your contact on the slide so people can follow up. Add your name and district email before the talk.

## Open questions for this section

- Add the real handout link and your contact details before presenting. Both are placeholders.
- Decide whether to end on the question (slide 5) or hold a hard call to action as the final image. The question invites engagement; a single "one task, one hour" slide is a stronger last image if you want them to leave with the instruction, not a discussion.
- If the session runs tight, slides 1 and 2 can merge into one recap slide.
